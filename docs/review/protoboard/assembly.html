Assembly order and how to solder a shared point

Do not wire the empty board first. Fit the low components, socket and header, then the XIAO, MOSFETs and large capacitor. Solder and trim each component lead before rear wires obstruct it. Leave U1 out of its socket and JP1 open.

Next install the five bare bridges. Then work through the four wire heights: 2.7, 4.6, 6.5 and 8.4 mm from the rear surface to the wire centre. Long cable tails are installed at their assigned height, not all at the end. Thread those that pass through the cap floor before attaching them, and park their free ends outside the work area.

At a shared point, the wire end sits against the existing component lead or copper pad. Strip only about 2 mm, lightly tin the strands, and hold it with tweezers while reheating the joint. Let it cool before releasing it. The guide lists the wires already attached there: hold them still and recheck them after reflow. Never twist several insulated wires into a tall bundle at the joint.

Use removable tape to hold the route during fitting. Do not tension wires, kink PVC, leave whiskers of copper, or press the board down onto a wire pile. Strain-relieve outgoing cables on the cap floor, away from the solder pads.

Wire fit, assumptions and checks

The owner’s Electronix Express kit is stranded 22 AWG. Amazon describes 0.010″ PVC insulation; that is not the overall diameter. Electronix Express lists approximately 1.7 mm overall diameter. Measure the actual wire before following this plan.

The existing routes are checked here at Ø1.7 mm, including their vertical rises. They leave at least 0.2 mm between unrelated wire corridors and only about 0.105 mm at the tightest unrelated solder joint. That is nominal clearance, not manufacturing tolerance. The old reference drawing used Ø1.6 mm and has slightly more clearance.

The highest wire’s outside surface reaches 9.25 mm into the 10 mm gap. There is only 0.75 mm left: no additional slack loop belongs above that route. The nominal tool-access check assumes a fine Ø1.8 mm tip approaching an unrelated joint straight from the rear. Real tip taper, solder volume and shared-joint crowding still need inspection.

The code checks endpoints, electrical continuity, screw clearances, wire crossings, terminal rises and the build order. It does not certify a PVC bend radius, actual wire sag, fan-out of several ends within a shared joint, or your soldering iron’s access. Stop if the route requires a sharp kink or squeezes another wire; inspect and revise it before soldering. Do not assume a numerical cut length proves a physical fit.

With power disconnected, check each finished wire with a meter, then inspect nearby pads. Capacitors and semiconductor paths mean an arbitrary continuity beep is not by itself proof of a short. Continue with the build guide’s staged power checks only after the mechanical dry fit.
