# Mechanical research: charging socket (twist-to-pour rotor), shells, handle, printing

Scope: return spring for the ~44 mm rotor (0 -> 45 deg -> spring back), heat-set inserts and screws, PETG fits and print settings, joining the big shells, magnet hatch, handle strength, bed-size check, food safety. Every number below comes from the linked page unless it is marked "estimate" or "calc". Amazon prices could not be read from the listing pages (the pages load but the price block is past the readable part), so Amazon prices are marked "not confirmed". Home Depot, Ace and Lowe's pages returned 403 on the fact-check pass, so their prices/SKUs are marked "(unverified)". See "Fact-check notes" at the end.

## Decisions / recommendations

**1. Return spring: primary = compression spring in a tangential pocket; alternate = small torsion spring on an M3 post. Magnets are a detent, not a return.**
- Primary: one stainless compression spring, 0.5 mm wire x 5 mm OD x 20 mm free length (fall back to 0.4 mm wire if it feels too stiff), sitting in a 5.6 mm wide printed pocket in the housing, pushing on a radial arm of the rotor at radius r = 10 mm from the rotor axis. 45 deg at r = 10 mm is 7.9 mm of arc travel (calc); with 2 mm preload the spring is compressed 9.9 mm at 45 deg, which the 20 mm spring can do (solid height about 8.5 mm, calc). Estimated rate for 0.5 x 5 x 20 (about 15 active coils) is 0.39 N/mm, giving about 0.8 N.cm at 0 deg and about 3.8 N.cm at 45 deg (calc); for 0.4 mm wire about 0.15 N/mm, 0.3 N.cm at 0 and 1.5 N.cm at 45 (calc). Both come in the Mardatt 240-pc kit [B09C1DDHRV](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09C1DDHRV) (0.3/0.4/0.5 mm wire, 4/5/6 mm OD, 10-50 mm lengths); a single-size pack is uxcell 0.4 x 5 x 20 [B076LZZQPW](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076LZZQPW). Cap the pocket so the spring cannot buckle sideways (pocket width = OD + 0.6 mm, depth = full free length + 1 mm, closed ends on both sides). Big-box fallback: Everbilt 84-pack spring assortment, Home Depot model 24470 (Store SKU 471864, $5.97 - unverified, Home Depot blocked the fetch; random sizes; expect to find one that works). Local same-day: see "Seattle-area stores" below.
- Alternate: torsion spring on an M3 screw post near the rotor rim, one leg on the housing wall, the other pushed by a rotor tab. McMaster 9271K17 (0.025 in wire, 0.235 in OD, 0.75 in legs, 0.402 in.lbf at 90 deg, steel, pack of 6) or the lighter 9271K13 (0.017 in wire, 0.16 in OD, 0.5 in legs, 0.125 in.lbf at 90 deg). At 45 deg 9271K17 gives about 2.3 N.cm at the spring; through its 19 mm leg on a rotor tab at r = 18 mm that is about 2.2 N.cm rotor torque (calc). A torsion spring AROUND the 44 mm rotor hub (the "wrap the hub" layout) is not practical: no stock spring has a 45 mm ID at sane torque, and a small hollow stub under the rotor floor collides with the A3144 module board, so skip that layout. Amazon kit if you want to try several: 120-pc stainless torsion kit [B0DPFVQJCC](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DPFVQJCC) (30 sizes; wire/OD list not readable on the page) or Dianrui 50-pc 45/90/120 deg [B0BVR3BYN8](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BVR3BYN8).
- Extension spring: workable (hook on a rotor pin and a housing pin, tangent to the rotor) but it needs two hook posts and the spring hooks fatigue in the wire; Beesini 100-pc stainless extension kit [B0CXJMNDDL](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CXJMNDDL) exists if you want it. Not recommended.
- Magnetic return with the owner's 6 x 2 magnets: two attracting 6 x 2 N35 magnets pull 1.10 lb (4.9 N) at contact, 0.40 lb at 1 mm, 0.21 lb at 2 mm, 0.12 lb at 3 mm, 0.05 lb at 5 mm (K&J calculator, see table; the gap values could not be re-run on the fact-check pass, the surface-field values were reproduced by formula). At the 44 mm rotor rim 45 deg separates the pair by about 17 mm of arc (chord 16.8 mm, calc), where the force is essentially zero, so magnets cannot return the rotor from 45 deg. A repelling magnet at the 45 stop only pushes back for the last 2-3 mm. Verdict: NOT feasible as the return; feasible and nice as a 0 deg "click" detent (one attracting pair at the 0 position, magnets touching through a 0.4 mm skin or face to face). Keep all return/detent magnets at least 25 mm from the A3144 (the 6 x 2 field is 373 G at 5 mm per the calculator; the A3144 is unipolar and a nearby latch magnet can mask or false-trigger it).
- Target feel: 2-5 N.cm at 45 deg with about 1 N.cm preload at 0 (estimate). Reference points: panel-mount rotary switches (CTS 227 series) are specified at 10-60 in.oz detent torque (7-42 N.cm); miniature PCB rotaries are well under 2 N.cm. The compression spring with 0.5 mm wire lands at the low end of the panel-switch range, which is what a ring twisted by one finger wants. Print the socket first and try both wire sizes.

**2. Heat-set inserts and screws**
- Insert: M3 x 5.7 mm long, 4.6 mm OD (ruthex RX-M3x5.7: D1 4.6, D2 3.9, D3 4.0, L 5.7, min wall 1.6 mm). Amazon: ruthex 100-pc [B08BCRZZS3](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08BCRZZS3) (price not confirmed; 3DJake lists the same pack at $8.39 excl. VAT). McMaster equivalent 94459A140 (M3 x 5.7 mm, brass, max hole 4 mm, min material thickness 6.5 mm, 50-pack).
- Pilot hole: 4.0 mm (-0/+0.1) in PETG (Accu chart; McMaster says max 4 mm). Print the hole at 4.0 mm, blind, 7 mm deep (insert + 1 mm for displaced plastic per CNC Kitchen), straight walls, no chamfer needed. Boss OD at least 8 mm (4.0 + 2 x 1.6 min wall, rounded up).
- Install: soldering iron at about 245 C for PETG (print temp + 10-20 C), flat/chisel or a dedicated insert tip; press slowly and straight until about 90 % in, then push the last bit flush with a flat tool and hold a few seconds so it does not creep back (CNC Kitchen). Do this with the part on the bed of the printer or a flat surface, let the plastic cool before threading a screw.
- Screws: 640-pc 304 stainless M3 socket-head kit [B0HKFCTLK8](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HKFCTLK8) (6/8/10/12/16/20/25/30 mm, with nuts and washers; price not confirmed). The socket needs 4 x M3 x 8; the sphere flange, hatch and handle use 8-12 mm.

**3. Fits, stops, retention, PETG rules**
- Rotor OD in housing bore: 0.25-0.30 mm radial clearance per side (0.5-0.6 mm on diameter). PETG strings and fuses more than PLA; a PETG-specific guide gives 0.25-0.35 mm for sliding faces and 0.30-0.40 mm for cylindrical axle fits. Start at 0.30 mm per side for the rotor and 0.25 mm for the ring key tab in its keyway; print a 20 mm tall test ring/bore coupon before the real socket.
- Alignment pins (hatch, shell halves): 0.15-0.20 mm per side, 3-4 mm diameter, 4-5 mm long, chamfered 0.5 mm (estimate; print and check).
- Hard stops: a 3 mm pin on the rotor riding in a 45 deg arc slot in the housing floor (slot width 3.6 mm, arc length = 45 deg + 0.5 mm end clearance each side). Better: make the "pin" an M3 x 8 screw threaded into a heat-set insert in the rotor so the stop part is steel and replaceable. The printed slot ends must have at least 2.4 mm of wall behind them. The microswitch must never be the stop: cam lobe trips the D2F-L at about 35-40 deg, the slot end takes the twist at 45.
- Rotor retention (so nobody yanks the rotor out with the ring): give the rotor a bottom flange 3 mm larger in radius than the bore, assemble it from below, and close the housing with the base plate (the one carrying the Hall sensor) using the 4 M3 inserts. Axial play 0.3 mm. The ring key itself is a bayonet: the key tab enters at 0 deg and the rotor returns to 0 before the ring can come out, so the ring is removable only at rest, which is what you want.
- No living hinges or flexing snap tabs in PETG for anything structural: PETG has 5 % elongation at yield (Prusament datasheet) and layer adhesion of 18 MPa, so a flexure printed across layers cracks after a few cycles. Use pins + magnets + screws instead.
- Bridging: keep unsupported bridges under 15 mm in PETG (Creality's guideline table: 25 mm PLA, 15 mm PETG), bridge speed 20-30 mm/s, fan 70-80 % on bridge layers. Design chamfers (45 deg) or 0.2 mm sacrificial layers over wide openings (service door, hatch) instead of long bridges.
- Print settings for the structural parts (Creality PETG, 0.4 nozzle, 0.2 mm layers is fine): nozzle 230-245 C (Prusa's PETG article: 230 first layer, 240 after; Prusament datasheet 250 +/- 10), bed 70-85 C (article 85-90 C; datasheet 80 +/- 10 C), fan OFF for the first 3-5 layers then 30-50 % (Prusa: fan off gives max toughness; 50 % per datasheet), 4 perimeters, 5 top/bottom layers, 25-30 % gyroid infill, 40-60 mm/s outer walls. Prusa: "for solid parts add perimeters rather than infill". Textured/powder-coated PEI sheet or glue stick on smooth PEI (PETG welds to smooth PEI). Do NOT anneal: Prusa's test gained impact durability only at 110 C and above, with dimensional change and warping that grow with temperature, and they call it unsuitable for complicated or detailed prints (only for simple parts that need strength).

**4. Joining the big shells**
- Default: do not glue the sphere halves; bolt them with an M3 flange (6 bosses with inserts around the equator, M3 x 10) so LEDs and nozzle stay serviceable. Glue only decorative trim, the ring cap, and the lower reflector to the base if you never need to open it.
- Permanent PETG-to-PETG bond: 2-part epoxy, J-B Weld ClearWeld 5-minute, 25 mL syringe 2-pack [B09C6R4VSQ](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09C6R4VSQ) (3900 psi, sets 5 min, cures 1 h, 25 mL per syringe per [jbweld.com 50112](https://www.jbweld.com/product/clearweld-syringe); Amazon price not confirmed). Scuff both faces with 120-220 grit, wipe with IPA, clamp.
- Fast tack / small parts (ring cap, magnet pockets, pins): medium CA + activator, Starbond 4 oz medium + 8 oz pump accelerator [B09Z36D448](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09Z36D448) (price not confirmed). CA is brittle on big joints; use it to hold while epoxy cures.
- Solvent welding: PETG IS attacked by acetone and MEK (rated 3 = unstable on the Brett Martin PETG chart), so MEK or dichloromethane will weld it, but they are hazardous, messy on a 180 mm seam and soften the surface finish. Skip for this build.
- Magnet hatch: 6 x 2 magnets in 6.2-6.3 mm diameter pockets, pocket depth 1.6 mm so the magnet stands 0.4 mm proud and the two magnets TOUCH when closed (force falls from 1.10 lb at contact to 0.40 lb at 1 mm, so any gap kills it). Two pairs = about 10 N holding (calc from the K&J value), plenty for a hatch. CA the magnets in, mark polarity with a Sharpie before gluing (attracting pairs), add two 3 mm alignment pins + a finger notch, and keep the pairs 25 mm+ from the A3144.

**5. Handle**
- A 14 x 10 mm PETG arch holding 2 kg is fine. Beam check (calc): 60 N (2 kg with a 3x jerk factor) on a 240 mm span gives a mid-span moment of 3.6 N.m, bending stress about 11 MPa in a 10 x 14 section versus 66 MPa flexural strength for printed PETG (Prusament datasheet). Safety factor about 6 even before the arch shape helps.
- Print each half lying flat (arch in the XY plane) so the bending load runs along the layers, 4 perimeters, 40 % infill (or 100 % for the 2 small parts; it is cheap). Interlayer strength is only 18 MPa, so never print the arch standing up.
- Lap joint: split the 10 mm thickness into two 5 mm laps over a 25-30 mm overlap, 2 x M3 x 10 screws into inserts plus epoxy. Put the joint 30-40 mm from one boss, not at the top where the moment peaks. The screws see about 70 N shear (calc), trivial.
- Metal core: not needed. If you want it anyway, Everbilt 1/4 in x 3 ft plain steel rod, Home Depot model 0001 (Store SKU 725602, $5.64 - unverified, fetch blocked), bent around a form into a 6.6 mm channel in the handle (its EI is about 4x the PETG section's, calc). The Everbilt 1/2 in x 3 ft x 1/8 in aluminum flat bar (model 6196; SKU 235295, $6.78 - unverified) would need to be bent edgewise to help, which you cannot do by hand; skip it.
- The handle hangs the whole 2 kg lantern from the sphere equator, so every vertical joint below the bosses (sphere halves, sphere-to-reflector, reflector-to-base) must be screwed with M3 flanges, not glued or magnet-held.

**6. Print bed check (parts up to 180 mm diameter x 95 mm tall)**
- Fits: Ender 3 (220 x 220 x 250), Prusa MK4S (250 x 210 x 220), Bambu A1 / P1S / X1C (256 x 256 x 256). On the MK4S and Ender 3 a 180 mm disc leaves only 15-20 mm per side, so use a skirt not a brim, and center it.
- Does not fit: Bambu A1 mini (180 x 180 x 180): a 180 mm part has zero margin and the slicer's edge exclusion makes it unprintable. Options: (a) split the sphere into 4 quarter shells and the base/reflectors into halves with alignment pins and M3 flanges, or (b) scale the whole lantern to 1.6x (sphere/base about 160 mm) which leaves 10 mm per side. Kevin's printer model is not in the brief; confirm it before finalizing the CAD split.

**7. Food safety**
- PETG prints must not touch the drink. Layer lines trap liquid and bacteria and cannot be cleaned, pigments and additives are unknown, and Prusa's own guidance is not to use FDM prints as food containers. Only the glass, the silicone tubing and the pump head touch the beverage; the printed glass bay is a holder only, with a removable drip tray.
- Spills: make the drip tray and the floor of the glass bay smooth (print face down on textured PEI, 0 % infill solid top), rinse with warm water and dish soap, do not use acetone or MEK (they attack PETG) and keep it away from the dishwasher (PETG softens around 68-85 C).

## Open questions / risks

- Kevin's printer model (and therefore bed size) is not in the brief. If it is an A1 mini, the 180 mm parts must be split or the model scaled to 1.6x; decide before the CadQuery split is frozen.
- Amazon prices could not be read from the listing pages; all Amazon prices above are estimates. McMaster shows no price without a login.
- The owner's magnets' grade is not stated (assumed N35). Hatch and detent forces scale about 30 % higher if they are N45.
- Spring rates for the kit springs are calculated, not from a datasheet (number of active coils unknown). Print the socket, try 0.4 and 0.5 mm wire, keep the one that returns crisply without making the ring hard to turn.
- Hall sensor vs. latch/detent magnets: any extra magnet near the A3144 (within roughly 25 mm) can mask or false-trigger the "ring present" signal. Keep the 0 deg detent pair at the rotor rim and verify with the firmware's serial debug before closing the sphere.
- PETG shrinkage and stringing vary by spool and printer; the 0.30 mm rotor clearance is a starting point. Budget one test coupon print (bore + rotor ring + key tab) before the full socket.
- The rotor's pin-in-slot stop is loaded every twist; if the printed slot end wears, swap to an M3 screw pin in an insert (designed in above) and add a 0.5 mm steel washer under the head.
- Ace Hardware, Lowe's and (on the fact-check pass) Home Depot pages could not be fetched (bot block), so the Hillman assortment contents are from search snippets only and the Everbilt prices/SKUs are unverified; the Everbilt model numbers are the only part that could be cross-checked (product URLs carry them).
