# Pump, tubing, reservoir, glass and fluid path

Research notes for the Green Lantern shot-pouring prop. Every number below is tagged with the page it came from; "estimate" means no page confirmed it, "(unverified)" means the fact-checker could not reach the page or find the number on it. NOTHING HAS BEEN BOUGHT YET (brief correction 2026-10-11): the Kamoer KPHM200 (B09HGVLHFV) and the JoyTube 1/8" x 3/16" tubing (B08RYFGNMK) are items on Kevin's Amazon shortlist, not parts in hand.

## Decisions / recommendations

- Keep the Kamoer KPHM200-SW3B16 (12 V brushed, 3 rollers, B16 tube 3.2 x 6.4 mm) as the primary pump. Drive it from the 12 V rail through the D4184 MOSFET module; budget 0.7 A (10 W) continuous, and allow about 2 A inrush in the fuse choice (inrush is an estimate, not published).
- Simpler alternative (single 5 V rail, about 2x slower): Adafruit 3910 "Peristaltic Liquid Pump with Silicone Tubing - 5V to 6V DC Power", $24.95, 500 mA, up to 100 mL/min, 3.5 mm ID x 5 mm OD silicone tube, 27.8 mm dia motor x 66.8 mm long, 104 g, self-priming ([Adafruit 3910](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3910)). A 30 mL pour takes about 18 s instead of 9 s, which is longer than the ~10 s light show; that is the trade.
- Do NOT put the JoyTube 1/8" x 3/16" tube in the pump head. Its wall is about 0.8 mm; the KPHM200 head is built for a 1.6 mm wall (6.4 mm OD). It will not be occluded by the rollers and will pump little or nothing. If bought at all, keep it only as spare inlet/outlet hose if a barb joint is ever needed.
- Buy AUBER platinum-cured silicone 1/8" ID x 1/4" OD, 10 ft (B0DKB1NKB4, $13.99). It is the exact 3.2 x 6.4 / 1.6 mm wall the head needs, is marked FDA 21 CFR 177.2600, USP Class VI and NSF 51, and 10 ft covers the first tube plus two spares.
- Run ONE continuous tube: reservoir dip tube -> through the pump head -> up to the nozzle. No barbs, no joints, no drip from a leaking fitting. Total length in the lantern is roughly 50-70 cm (estimate from the chosen 1.8x lantern layout). Cut the nozzle end square; the bare silicone tube end is the nozzle. A stainless barb (MAACFLOW B0CFQJXTN6) is listed only as an optional rigid nozzle insert / splice.
- Silicone is fine for 35 % ABV Jagermeister: platinum silicone rates "good" (2 of 4) for ethyl alcohol in the BNL tubing chart, PharMed BPT rates "excellent" (1). Kamoer's own pages rate the silicone tube at 200 h life vs 1000 h for BPT. At a few minutes of pumping per party, 200 h is never reached; replace the tube yearly or when it looks cloudy or flattened.
- Sugar: Jagermeister is a sugared herbal liqueur (the exact g/100 mL figure could not be verified, see Fact-check notes). Dried residue gums the tube bore and glues the rollers' track. Always flush with warm water after the party and store the tube dry and OUT of the rollers (release the quick-release head cap).
- Reservoir: 8 oz (250 mL) clear PET Boston round, 24-410 neck, 2.25 in (57 mm) dia x 5.219 in (133 mm) tall (ContainerAndPackaging B320 spec). It fits the 60 x 150 mm pocket with a 24-410 cap on top. Drill the PP cap with two 6 mm holes: tube pass-through (silicone tube self-seals in a 6 mm hole; a 6 mm ID top-hat grommet is optional) and a 2-3 mm vent hole. Flexible-pouch alternative: 8 oz food-grade "plastic flask" pouches (B0B8VQNJVR) collapse as they empty so no vent is needed, but they do not hold the dip tube in place and are harder to swap at a party; keep as plan B.
- Shot glass: Libbey 92440 Infinium Torque 1.5 oz, Tritan plastic, H 2 1/8 in (54 mm), top 1 7/8 in (48 mm), base 1 1/2 in (38 mm). Fits the 55 x 80 mm bay with 26 mm left above the rim for the nozzle. Glass alternative with the same footprint: Libbey 5120 1.5 oz (60 mm tall, 51 mm top). Skip 2 oz "shooter" shapes; the Libbey 5109 shooter is 89 mm tall and others are taller still.
- Serving: pour 30 mL (1 oz) by default. At the rated 200 mL/min that is 9.0 s, which matches the ~10 s light show, and leaves 14 mL headroom in a 44 mL glass so nothing sloshes when he lifts it. Calibrate with the real liqueur; expect slightly less than 200 mL/min because of viscosity and the ~15-20 cm lift to the nozzle.
- Firmware: hard max pump run 15 s, 1-2 s of reverse is NOT available (brushed motor, single direction through the D4184), so stop drips by nozzle placement, not by suck-back.
- Nozzle: tube end 10-15 mm above the glass rim, pointed at the inside wall of the glass, held by a printed clip so the last drop lands in the glass. A small removable drip tray under the glass is still worth printing (one printed part, zero cost); the stopped peristaltic head holds the column in the tube, but the last drop at the open nozzle end can still fall.
- Priming: fill bottle, run pump 4-6 s until liquid reaches the nozzle (peristaltic pumps are self-priming by principle), discard that first pour. After priming the tube stays full; subsequent pours start within ~0.2 s.

### Pour math (rated 200 mL/min = 3.33 mL/s; 260 mL/min reference = 4.33 mL/s)

| Volume | Time at 200 mL/min | Time at 260 mL/min | Time at 100 mL/min (Adafruit 3910) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 mL | 4.5 s | 3.5 s | 9.0 s |
| 30 mL (1 oz) | 9.0 s | 6.9 s | 18.0 s |
| 44 mL (1.5 oz) | 13.2 s | 10.2 s | 26.4 s |

Recommendation: 30 mL default, firmware constant `POUR_MS` starts at 9000 and is set by calibration (weigh three pours). If Kevin wants a full 1.5 oz in ~10 s and the pump really does 260 mL/min, 44 mL works, but then a 1.5 oz glass is brim-full; a taller 2 oz glass needs the bay raised to about 90 mm.

## Priming, drips, cleaning, storage

1. Tube routing: dip tube reaches to about 5 mm above the bottle bottom; bottle sits upright in the base; tube goes up through the lower reflector neck, through the pump head (pump mounted with the head face toward the service door so the quick-release cap can be opened), then up to the nozzle clip above the glass. Keep the pump below the nozzle; keep all tube bends larger than about 25 mm radius so the 1.6 mm-wall silicone does not kink (estimate).
2. Priming: with the glass in place, hold a service button (or use the ring twice) for 4-6 s until liqueur reaches the nozzle; discard. The head is self-priming; a half metre of lift is well within reach of this class of pump.
3. Nozzle: 10-15 mm above the rim, aimed at the inside wall of the glass; this hides splash and lands the last drop. Because the rollers hold the column in the tube, the only possible drip is what hangs at the open end, so the drip tray is a 2-minute print, not a requirement.
4. Pour: run `POUR_MS` (start 9000 ms for 30 mL), then stop. No reverse. Removing the ring cuts the MOSFET gate immediately.
5. Cleaning (same night): swap the bottle for one with warm tap water, run the pump 30 s into a cup, repeat once, then run 10 s with the dip tube out of the water to push air through and empty the line. Pop the quick-release head cap and lift the tube off the rollers. Air dry with both tube ends open. Do not store sugary liqueur in the tube.
6. Between parties: leave the tube out of the rollers (or at least open the head cap) so the silicone is not held crushed for months; the pinch clamp goes on only when transporting a full bottle, never on a wet tube in storage.
7. Every season: inspect the tube inside the head for a flat spot or cloudiness and replace it (10 ft of AUBER gives several spares).

## Open questions / risks

- KPHM200 motor diameter, pump head diameter and mounting hole pattern are not published on any reachable page (Kamoer's manual needs a kamoer.com login; DirectIndustry and robu.in block fetches). When the pump arrives, measure overall L, head width/height, motor diameter, screw hole spacing and screw thread (likely 3.5 mm clearance holes for M3 by analogy with the KPHM100's 4 x dia 3.5 mm pattern) before the pump bracket is finalised. The CAD bracket should be parametric and clamp the motor body rather than depend on the hole pattern.
- Flow is listed as 200 mL/min in the Amazon bullet and 260 mL/min as the reference flow in the Amazon spec table (and in robu.in's title); Jagermeister is more viscous than water and the nozzle is 15-20 cm above the pump. Real flow must be measured; 30 mL may take 8-11 s.
- Whether the tube in the head survives being "switch controlled" with PWM at 9-10 V: Kamoer lists PWM only for the brushless variant. Running the brushed motor on PWM is normal practice but is Kevin's risk; keep PWM frequency above ~200 Hz or at 20 kHz+ to avoid buzz (estimate).
- The AUBER "FDA registered" / USP Class VI claims are seller statements; a lot certificate was not found. For a party prop this is adequate; for peace of mind the Kamoer BPT tube is the manufacturer's "excellent for ethanol" option, but its pre-cut length for the KPHM200 head is unconfirmed.
- Kamoer's silicone tube life of 200 h is for continuous pumping; residue from sugary liqueur, not wear, is the realistic failure mode. Cleaning discipline matters more than the tube brand.
- Stopped-roller position: the patents note a roller stopping exactly at the track entry/exit can let fluid creep; with the nozzle above the glass and the reservoir below the pump, any creep goes back down to the bottle, not out of the nozzle.
- Amazon returned HTTP 500 for several listings (kelkaa bottle, HORTIPOTS grommet, Drinique glass, flip-cap bottle) on every attempt, so those stay "(unverified)". Both reservoir and glass have confirmed alternatives (ContainerAndPackaging B320; Libbey 92440 at $69.99/24 and 5120 at $21.99/12 on WebstaurantStore).
