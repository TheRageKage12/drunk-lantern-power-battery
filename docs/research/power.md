# Power system research (USB power bank -> 5 V + 12 V)

Scope: pump (Kamoer KPHM200, 12 V), XIAO RP2040 + WS2812B LEDs (5 V), everything from one USB power bank in the lantern base. All numbers below come from the linked pages; anything not confirmed is labeled "estimate" or "(unverified)". Fact-checked 2026-10-11 (see notes at the end).

## Decisions / recommendations

- **Topology: ONE 5 V port feeds everything.** Bank USB-A (5 V/3 A) -> USB-A pigtail -> master switch -> fuse -> 5 V bus (XIAO 5V pin, NeoPixel ring, LED strip, 1000 uF cap). A 5 V -> 12 V step-up cable hangs off the same 5 V bus and feeds the D4184 MOSFET module (DC+/DC-) which switches the pump. Ground is common by construction (the boost cable is non-isolated). Do NOT use two ports of the bank at once (see common-ground section: a 10K bank throttles / drops PD when a second port is loaded).
- **12 V source, primary = option (b), a USB 5 V -> 12 V step-up cable.** First choice is [B0CF5DR1JJ](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CF5DR1JJ) (wininks; "Output DC 12V 1.2A MAX", "Input USB 5V 2-3A", "Do not use the device for more than 14 watts", 5.5x2.1 mm plug, 5 ft) **but the listing showed "Currently unavailable" when fetched on 2026-10-11**, so buy the [HiLetgo 3-pack B073R7Z7CL](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B073R7Z7CL) (12 V, "Max Power 10W", 5.5x2.1 mm, 1 m, $7.99 for 3) and keep the 1.2 A cable as the upgrade if it comes back in stock. The pump is rated 12 V / 0.7 A (8.4 W) on its Amazon listing, so the 10 W HiLetgo has ~1.6 W of margin and the 14 W cable has plenty. Cut the barrel plug off and solder to the D4184 DC+/DC- after checking polarity with a meter (neither listing states center-positive).
- **12 V fallback = option (c), MT3608 boost module** ([Dorhea 10-pack B0BGLGL9RV](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BGLGL9RV), $7.99 for 10, "DC 2-24V input and 5V-28V output", 2 A current rating, "up to 93%" efficiency). The 2 A figure is the module's rating; treat anything above ~1.2 A continuous as needing a heatsink (common builder advice, not on the listing). Set to 12.0 V with a meter BEFORE connecting the pump. Use only if the cable sags under pump inrush. XL6009 ([B07L64GJ42](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07L64GJ42), 6-pack, "Input Voltage: 3V-32V", "Input Current: 4A (max)", efficiency "up to 94%") also works but is larger.
- **Option (a), USB-C PD 12 V trigger cable, is NOT the primary.** It only works on a bank that advertises a 12 V PDO. The Anker PowerCore 10000 PD (A1235) advertises 5 V/3 A, 9 V/2 A, 15 V/1.25 A and no 12 V ([Chargerlab A1235 teardown](https://www.chargerlab.com/anker-powercore-10000-pd-power-bank-a1235-in-depth-review-compact-wonder/)), and the Anker 313 chosen below has no PD at all; a fixed-12 V cable on such a bank "will provide 5V or some other lower voltage" ([Adafruit 5450 notes](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5450)). If you ever move to a bigger LED build (>15 W), the path is: INIU B61 (USB-C PDO 12 V/1.5 A) + [wininks 12 V trigger cable B0FD9P7PV4](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FD9P7PV4) ($9.97, "Voltage range: 11.8V-12.2V", "Power: Max 36W constant", "Centre pin positive", 100 cm) on the USB-C port ONLY, then a 12 V -> 5 V buck for the LEDs. That is a bigger redesign; not needed for 84 LEDs.
- **Power bank: Anker 313 Power Bank (PowerCore 10K / "PowerCore Slim 10000"), model A1229** ([Amazon B0D5CLSMFB](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D5CLSMFB), $21.99). 10,000 mAh, "5V / 3A High-Speed Charging", "Two-Way USB-C Charging: The USB-C port supports both input and output functions", connectors "USB-C*1+USB-A*1", and a documented **trickle-charging mode** (Amazon spec table; Anker manual: press the power button twice or hold 2 s, LED turns green, press once to exit). **Size per the Amazon listing is 6.01 x 2.82 x 0.61 in (152.7 x 71.6 x 15.5 mm), 240 g / 8.48 oz** - NOT the 149.5 x 68.5 x 14.5 mm / 206.5 g in the older Anker A1229 manual. Make the base pocket ~156 x 75 x 25 mm plus ~25 mm cable clearance at the port end. The older A1229 unit (manual + anker.com) has USB-A output 5 V/2.4 A only (12 W) with USB-C input only; if the unit you receive is labeled 5 V/2.4 A, cap LEDs to 20 % during the pour.
- **Low-current auto-off: this build is probably safe even without trickle mode, but design for it anyway.** Banks cut output when load drops below roughly 50-200 mA. The lantern idles at ~0.13 A (XIAO ~50 mA + 84 WS2812B at ~1 mA each when dark); keep a faint idle "pilot" glow on the 24-px ring (~0.1 A extra) so idle draw is ~0.25 A, comfortably above any threshold. Trickle mode is the belt-and-braces backup. Mount the bank with its power button reachable through the service door (printed plunger or cut-out).
- **Current budget / runtime:** worst realistic peak is ~2.9 A at 5 V during a pour (pump 2.0 A reflected + LEDs at 50 %). Firmware should cap LED brightness at ~40-50 % during POURING and never do full-white on the 60-LED strip (3.6 A by itself). Expect ~26-32 h idle (0.2-0.25 A), ~10 h armed-pulsing, and a full Halloween night (4 h glow + 30 pours) uses ~2.3 Ah of the ~6.5 Ah usable at 5 V (estimate).
- **Protection and switching:** master switch on the 5 V line right after the USB-A pigtail (KCD1 rocker, 19 x 13 mm cutout, 6 A (unverified listing), or Adafruit 916 16 mm latching metal button with LED, 3 A). Fuse the 5 V bus with a 5x20 mm **3 A slow-blow (T3A)** (the bank itself limits at ~3 A so the fuse is wiring/cap-short insurance); fuse the 12 V pump leg with **1 A slow-blow (T1A)**. 1000 uF 10 V across the 5 V bus at the LED feed; 1000 uF 16 V across the 12 V rail at the D4184 input (absorbs motor inrush). 22 AWG silicone wire everywhere; 20 AWG for the main 5 V trunk if > 0.5 m. JST-SM 3-pin for ring and strip, Dupont for Hall and microswitch.
- **Charging without opening the lantern:** panel-mount USB-C extension (female on the base rear, male plugged permanently into the bank's USB-C port, which is input+output on the current unit). Whether this bank passes through (charges and discharges at once) is not documented on the Anker pages fetched (unverified), so charge with the master switch OFF. The bank charges at 5 V/2 A (manual and anker.com); ~5 h from empty is an estimate (Anker gives no figure).
- **Firmware adds for power:** PWM soft-start on the D4184 PWM pin (ramp 0 -> 100 % over ~300 ms) to tame inrush through the boost cable; brightness cap during POURING; idle pilot glow; "pump off at boot" already required.

## Common-ground check

- Inside any single power bank all output ports share the battery negative; PD negotiation changes only VBUS on the USB-C port. Tying the GND of a 12 V PD output to the GND of a 5 V USB-A output of the same bank is electrically fine (no isolation to break, no loop). I found no manufacturer page that states this explicitly; it follows from the single-cell construction and from the "two-way independent design" measurement on the Anker A1235 where both ports were loaded together (13.97 W + 10.71 W) ([Chargerlab](https://www.chargerlab.com/anker-powercore-10000-pd-power-bank-a1235-in-depth-review-compact-wonder/)).
- The real problem is power sharing, not ground: on the INIU B61 only one port can be driven at full power; with several ports in use the output is heavily throttled and fast-charge modes are disabled ([techtest.org](https://techtest.org/iniu-powerbank-klein-aber-stark-22-5w-10000mah/)), i.e. the 12 V PDO would drop to 5 V the moment the USB-A port is loaded. Behaviour is model-specific and undocumented on most 10K banks. Hence: single-port design (chosen), common ground automatic.
- In the chosen design the boost cable, D4184 module, XIAO and LEDs all return to the same 5 V GND. Keep the pump wires twisted and routed away from the LED data line and Hall sensor; put the 12 V cap at the D4184.

## Wiring summary

```
Bank USB-A (5V/3A) --pigtail--> MASTER SWITCH --> FUSE T3A --> 5V BUS (+1000uF 10V)
   5V BUS --> XIAO 5V pin, AHCT125 VCC, A3144 VCC
   5V BUS --> JST-SM --> 24-px ring (inject at ring), 60-LED strip (inject both ends)
   5V BUS --> 5V->12V step-up cable --> FUSE T1A --> D4184 DC+/DC- (+1000uF 16V) --> OUT+/OUT- --> pump
   XIAO GPIO --> D4184 PWM/TRIG (soft-start PWM), D4184 GND --> common GND
Bank USB-C port <-- panel-mount USB-C extension (charge with master switch OFF)
```

## Open questions / risks

- KPHM200 current: 0.7 A at 12 V is on the Amazon listing bullet (8.4 W); the "10 W" figure is only from an unfetchable retailer page. Kamoer sells the KPHM200 with brushed, brushless or stepper motors - the Amazon B09HGVLHFV unit is the plain 12 V DC-motor version. Measure the pump on the bench; if it exceeds ~0.8 A running on the 10 W HiLetgo cable, go to the MT3608 module or the 14 W cable.
- Motor inrush through a 10-14 W boost cable is unverified. Mitigation: 1000 uF 16 V on the 12 V rail + PWM soft-start. If the cable brown-outs, the fallback module is on the BOM.
- Anker 313 revisions: the current Amazon listing (B0D5CLSMFB) is model A1229 with two-way USB-C and 5 V/3 A, 152.7 x 71.6 x 15.5 mm, 240 g; the older A1229 manual/anker.com unit is 5 V/2.4 A USB-A-only output, USB-C input only, 149.5 x 68.5 x 14.5 mm. Same model number, different hardware. Confirm the received unit's label says 5 V/3 A and that USB-C outputs; if it is the 2.4 A unit, cap LEDs to 20 % during the pour. Design the pocket for the larger unit.
- Trickle mode duration: Anker does not publish whether trickle mode times out; one blog reports another brand's low-current mode shutting off after "a couple of hours". The idle pilot glow (~0.25 A total) is the primary defence; verify on the bench that the bank stays on for 2+ h with the lantern idle.
- Does the bank auto-wake when the master switch applies load? Most do; if not, the power button must be reachable (design the bank pocket with button access anyway).
- Polarity of the 5 V -> 12 V cables is not stated on the pages fetched: check with a meter before wiring.
- Panel-mount USB-C flange dimensions and screw spacing were not on the page; measure the part before cutting the base (estimate: flange ~25 x 12 mm, screws ~20 mm apart).
- If the LED count grows (e.g. 144-LED strip), the 15 W single-port budget is gone; that is the point to switch to a 12 V/1.5 A PDO bank (INIU B61) + 12 V trigger cable + 12 V -> 5 V buck, or to a 45 W bank with a 15 V/20 V trigger and two bucks.
